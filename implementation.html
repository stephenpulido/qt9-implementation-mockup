What "success" means at QT9

1) Go-live readiness (no surprises)

A successful implementation is more than configuration. It is a go-live-ready production environment with trained process owners, validated workflows, and a cutover plan.

Success criteria
  • Critical workflows run end-to-end in your production environment
  • Process owners are trained and can execute daily transactions confidently
  • Go-live plan is approved and milestones are complete

2) Data integrity + traceability you can audit

If you cannot trace it, you cannot defend it. QT9 implementations focus on clean master data and auditable transaction paths.

Success criteria
  • Master data is validated: items, suppliers, customers, BOMs, routings, and units
  • Transactions flow cleanly from purchasing through inventory, production, and shipping
  • Records and approvals support FDA and ISO expectations

3) Adoption (teams actually use it)

If teams avoid the system, the project is not successful. Adoption is driven by clear ownership and role-based training.

Success criteria
  • Named process owners complete training
  • Standard workflows replace spreadsheets and workarounds
  • Exceptions and blockers decrease week over week

4) Stability after go-live

Go-live is the start line. A successful implementation stabilizes quickly.

Success criteria
  • Critical issues trend down during the first 30–60 days
  • Teams can close the month and run production without recurring blockers
  • Reporting and dashboards are in use for day-to-day decisions

How we measure implementation success

We track a simple implementation scorecard across four categories.

Delivery discipline

  • Follow-up on-time rate (Action Date met)
  • Overdue follow-ups (leading risk indicator)
  • Decision cycle time (how fast key project decisions get made and documented)

Readiness

  • Go-live target captured (every project has a date)
  • Cutover checklist completion (completed by defined milestones)
  • Core workflow validation pass rate (critical workflows executed end-to-end)

Adoption

  • Process owner training completion
  • Workflow sign-off coverage (required sign-offs completed by area)

Stability

  • Time to stabilize (days from go-live to steady-state)
  • Critical issue rate (blocked workflows during the first 30 days)
